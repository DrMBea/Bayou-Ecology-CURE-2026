# Low-Bandwidth Backup Activity: Louisiana Wetland Data Walkthrough

## Course
**ECOL 404B Ecology**  
**Project:** Where Bayous Meet Bytes - Ecology CURE

## Purpose
This activity is for students who cannot access CloudBank, Jupyter notebooks, or stable internet during the assigned activity window. You will use the same ecological reasoning as the notebook activity, but you will complete the work using a downloaded dataset, screenshots, printed handout, or copied table.

This is not the "lesser" assignment. It is the backup route. The science still matters.

## Student Learning Goals
By the end of this activity, you should be able to:

1. Identify ecological variables in a small Louisiana wetland dataset.
2. Describe patterns between environmental conditions and biological responses.
3. Interpret a simple table or graph using ecological reasoning.
4. Explain what additional data would improve the investigation.
5. Document any AI or digital tool support used during the activity.

## Scenario
Louisiana wetlands are affected by changing temperature, salinity, nutrients, dissolved oxygen, and water movement. These conditions can influence aquatic organisms, algal growth, biodiversity, and ecosystem health.

You are part of a student ecology research team reviewing wetland monitoring data. Your job is to make sense of the patterns and decide what questions the data raise.

## Part 1: Dataset Orientation
Use the provided table or file: `data/louisiana_wetland_sample_data.csv`.

3. Which variables are biological responses?

## Part 2: Pattern Hunt
Choose two variables to compare. Examples:

- Temperature and dissolved oxygen
- Salinity and habitat type
- Nitrate and chlorophyll-a
- Turbidity and algal cell count

Answer:

1. What two variables did you choose?
2. Why did you choose them?
3. What pattern do you notice?
4. Does the pattern support what you expected from ecology? Why or why not?
5. What might explain the pattern?
