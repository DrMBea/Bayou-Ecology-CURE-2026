# Where Bayous Meet Bytes: FacultyHack@Gateways 2026 Curriculum Project

## Repository Description
A beginner-friendly Ecology CURE repository supporting biology majors as they explore Louisiana ecological datasets using NAIRR CloudBank Classroom, Jupyter notebooks, Science Gateways, and guided computational workflows.

---

## Project Overview
This curriculum project modifies **ECOL 404B Ecology**, an upper-level course for biology majors at **Southern University and A&M College**. The primary goal is to integrate **NAIRR CloudBank Classroom**, guided **Jupyter notebook** activities, and Science Gateway-supported tutorials into a course-based undergraduate research experience (CURE) focused on Louisiana ecology.

The project is designed to help students move from learning ecology as course content to practicing ecology as an active research process. Students will investigate authentic ecological questions connected to Louisiana wetlands, biodiversity, climate change, water quality, environmental stressors, and coastal change. Through scaffolded activities, students will ask research questions, explore datasets, visualize ecological patterns, interpret results, and communicate findings.

This project intentionally uses beginner-friendly, browser-based tools so biology majors can build confidence with computational ecology without needing advanced programming or high-performance computing experience at the start of the course.

## Science Gateway Resources & Technology Notes
This curriculum module uses NAIRR CloudBank Classroom as the primary student-facing cloud workspace. The supporting tools and tutorials are selected to keep the first implementation realistic for biology majors while still introducing students to computational ecology, reproducible workflows, and Science Gateway-supported learning.

### Tools Used

**NAIRR CloudBank Classroom** [[CloudBank Classroom]](https://www.cloudbank.org/training/nairr-pilot-cloudbank-classroom)  
* **Description:** CloudBank Classroom is a cloud-hosted JupyterHub environment designed for teaching. It provides browser-based access to tools such as JupyterLab/Notebook and supports persistent student storage and compute resources.
* **Course Use:** CloudBank Classroom will serve as the main student workspace for storing project files, opening guided Jupyter notebooks, exploring ecological datasets, and completing beginner-friendly computational activities at no cost to students.

**Jupyter Notebooks / JupyterLab** [[Jupyter]](https://jupyter.org/)  
* **Description:** Jupyter notebooks allow text, code, data, visualizations, and reflection prompts to be combined in one interactive document.
* **Course Use:** Students will use guided notebooks to explore ecological datasets, create basic visualizations, interpret environmental patterns, and connect computational output to ecological reasoning.

**Cornell Virtual Workshop** [[Cornell Virtual Workshop]](https://cvw.cac.cornell.edu/)  
* **Description:** Cornell Virtual Workshop provides self-paced training resources for cloud computing, data analysis, and high-performance computing concepts.
* **Course Use:** Selected tutorials will support student onboarding and instructor preparation by introducing cloud-computing concepts, basic workflow expectations, and background knowledge needed for computational ecology activities.

**HPC Carpentry** [[HPC Carpentry]](https://www.hpc-carpentry.org/lessons/)  
* **Description:** HPC Carpentry provides teaching materials that introduce learners to high-performance computing concepts, workflow management, and reproducible computational practices.
* **Course Use:** HPC Carpentry resources will be used selectively to support reproducible workflows, data organization, file management, and computational habits that students can carry into future research experiences.

**Louisiana-Focused Environmental and Ecological Datasets**  
* **Potential Sources:** [NOAA Digital Coast](https://coast.noaa.gov/digitalcoast/), [USGS Water Data for the Nation](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/), [GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/), and instructor-created or simulated datasets.
* **Course Use:** Students will use accessible datasets connected to wetlands, water quality, biodiversity, climate, and coastal change. Early activities may use simplified or simulated datasets before students move into larger public datasets.

### Implementation Notes

* **Session 1:** Refined the Ecology CURE focus, identified student needs, and clarified that the first student-facing platform should be beginner-friendly, browser-based, and realistic for biology majors.
* **Session 2:** Selected NAIRR CloudBank Classroom as the primary student workspace and identified Jupyter notebooks as the main format for guided data exploration, visualization, and reflection.
* **Session 3:** Identified Cornell Virtual Workshop and HPC Carpentry as support resources for student onboarding, instructor preparation, reproducible workflows, and low-barrier computational habits.
* **Session 4:** Drafted realistic project goals and Science Gateway goals centered on CloudBank-supported ecological data exploration rather than advanced HPC use as the first step.
* **Session 5:** Planned supporting course materials, including a student team charter, low-bandwidth backup options, guided worksheets, and milestone check-ins for the CURE.

## Course Implementation Plan

Students will work in small research teams as part of an Ecology CURE. Each team will select or be assigned an ecological question connected to Louisiana ecosystems. Teams will use CloudBank-hosted Jupyter notebooks and guided datasets to explore ecological patterns and communicate their findings.

### Proposed CURE Workflow

1. **Team Formation and Charter**  
   Students develop a team charter that defines roles, communication expectations, responsibilities, decision-making, and conflict-resolution strategies.

2. **Ecological Question Development**  
   Students identify a focused research question connected to Louisiana wetlands, biodiversity, climate, water quality, or coastal change.

3. **Dataset Exploration**  
   Students use instructor-curated, simulated, or public datasets to examine ecological variables and patterns.

4. **Guided Notebook Activity**  
   Students complete a Jupyter notebook that walks them through basic data cleaning, visualization, and interpretation.

5. **Analysis and Interpretation**  
   Students connect data patterns to ecological concepts and real-world environmental concerns.

6. **Scientific Communication**  
   Students present their findings through a short research presentation, poster, infographic, or written research summary.

7. **Reflection and Revision**  
   Students reflect on their research process, teamwork, use of digital tools, and growth as developing scientists.
