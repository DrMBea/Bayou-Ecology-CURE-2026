## Abstract

This project redesigns **ECOL 404B: Ecology in a Digital Age**, a Face-2-Face upper-level biology course at Southern University and A&M College. The redesign integrates **NAIRR CloudBank Classroom**, guided **Jupyter notebooks**, **Cornell Virtual Workshop**, and **HPC Carpentry** into a beginner-friendly course-based undergraduate research experience (**CURE**) focused on Louisiana ecology.

Students investigate ecological questions related to wetlands, biodiversity, climate change, water quality, and coastal change using browser-based tools that lower technical barriers while strengthening data literacy. The redesign preserves the course's creative, student-centered identity--virtual fieldwork, AI-supported inquiry, and science communication--while adding a clearer workflow for data exploration, visualization, interpretation, and final presentation.

The central lesson is that Science Gateway adoption in biology courses works best when the tools are accessible, scaffolded, and clearly tied to ecological reasoning.

---

## Introduction

ECOL 404B is an upper-level Ecology course for biology majors at Southern University and A&M College. The Fall 2026 offering is Face-2-Face and organized around the theme **"Where Bayous Meet Bytes."**

The course already emphasizes Louisiana ecosystems, ecological reasoning, creativity, and digital learning. This redesign adds a clearer CURE structure and a beginner-friendly Science Gateway workflow so students can move from learning ecology as content to practicing ecology as inquiry.

The motivation is to build student confidence with data, cloud tools, and responsible AI use without overwhelming learners who are new to computational work.

## Science Gateway Resources Used

- **NAIRR CloudBank Classroom** - cloud-hosted Jupyter environment; primary student-facing workspace for storage, browser-based access, and guided notebook activities.
- **Jupyter Notebooks / JupyterLab** - interactive notebooks for data exploration, visualization, and interpretation.
- **Cornell Virtual Workshop** - self-paced onboarding tutorials for cloud workflows and computing concepts.
- **HPC Carpentry** - reproducible workflow support, file organization, and computational habits.
- **Datasets** - NOAA Digital Coast, USGS Water Data, GBIF, plus instructor-created or simulated Louisiana ecology datasets.

These resources fit the course because they are browser-based, beginner-friendly, and support ecological reasoning without requiring advanced programming experience.

## Final Project / Deliverable

**Name of final project:** Digital Bayou Showcase CURE Project

---

## Lessons Learned

- Accessibility matters: a low-barrier, browser-based platform is the best entry point for beginning users.
- Scaffolding matters: tutorials, guided notebooks, and backup materials reduce friction.
- Learning goals must lead: ecological questions stay at the center while technology supports inquiry.

## Course Redesign: Before & After

### Initial Syllabus

- Strong ecological content, virtual fieldwork, and AI-supported learning were already present.
- Creative assignments and discussion-based engagement supported asynchronous learning.
- A Science Gateway workflow and explicit CURE sequence were not yet clearly specified.

### Modified Syllabus

- Adds a beginner-friendly CURE centered on Louisiana datasets and team-based inquiry.
- Places CloudBank Classroom, Jupyter notebooks, Cornell Virtual Workshop, and HPC Carpentry into the course sequence.
- Uses guided data exploration, low-bandwidth backups, and a final Digital Bayou Showcase to align assessment with the redesign.
