# CURE Module Overview: Where Bayous Meet Bytes

## Course
**ECOL 404B Ecology**  
**Audience:** Upper-level biology majors  
**Institution:** Southern University and A&M College  
**Project Context:** FacultyHack@Gateways 2026

## Module Purpose
This CURE module introduces biology majors to ecological research through Louisiana-focused datasets, guided data exploration, and beginner-friendly computational workflows. The goal is not to turn Ecology students into programmers overnight. The goal is to help them think like ecologists, ask better questions, work with evidence, and communicate what the data suggest.

Students will use **NAIRR CloudBank Classroom** as the primary student-facing workspace for file storage and guided **Jupyter notebook** activities. **Cornell Virtual Workshop** and **HPC Carpentry** tutorials will support onboarding and reproducible workflow habits.

## Driving Question
How can ecological data help us understand environmental change in Louisiana ecosystems?

## Student Learning Outcomes
By the end of this module, students will be able to:

1. Develop a focused ecological research question connected to Louisiana ecosystems.
2. Use a guided dataset to explore ecological variables such as temperature, dissolved oxygen, salinity, nutrients, turbidity, biodiversity, or population patterns.
3. Create basic visualizations that support ecological interpretation.
4. Explain what the data suggest, what the data do not prove, and what additional evidence would be needed.
5. Use digital tools responsibly by documenting tool use, protecting student data, and maintaining human oversight of interpretations.
6. Communicate findings through a short research presentation, poster, infographic, or written research summary.

## CURE Structure

### Phase 1: Team Formation and Research Identity
Students form research teams and complete a team charter. The charter defines roles, communication methods, responsibilities, response times, conflict-resolution steps, and expectations for equitable contribution.

### Phase 2: Ecological Question Development
Students review sample topics connected to Louisiana ecology and select or refine a research question. Possible areas include wetlands, coastal change, water quality, biodiversity, climate stressors, or environmental justice.

### Phase 3: Dataset Exploration
Students use an instructor-curated, simulated, or public dataset to examine ecological patterns. Early datasets should be intentionally small and clean so students can focus on ecological interpretation before moving into larger public datasets.

### Phase 4: Guided Jupyter Notebook Activity
Students complete a CloudBank-hosted notebook that walks them through loading a dataset, viewing variables, generating basic plots, and responding to ecological interpretation prompts.

### Phase 5: Interpretation and Scientific Communication
Students connect their data patterns to ecological principles and real-world Louisiana concerns. Teams communicate their findings using a short presentation, research poster, infographic, or written summary.

### Phase 6: Reflection and Revision
Students reflect on their research process, teamwork, data decisions, tool use, and growth as emerging scientists.

## Responsible AI and Digital Tool Expectations
Students may use approved AI or digital tools to support brainstorming, organization, coding explanations, or writing feedback. Students may not use AI to fabricate data, invent sources, replace their own interpretation, or submit unverified output as fact. Any AI or digital tool use must be disclosed in the final submission.

Minimum disclosure statement:

> I used [tool name] to support [specific task]. I reviewed, revised, and verified the output, and the final interpretation represents my own understanding.

## Instructor Notes
Start small. The first version of this CURE should prioritize student confidence, ecological reasoning, and successful completion over technical complexity. Advanced HPC concepts can be introduced later as enrichment once students understand the ecological workflow.
